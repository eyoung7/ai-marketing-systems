# Quality Control Checklist

What to actually check in AI-assisted marketing output before it ships — the specific failure modes, not a general "review for quality."

- [ ] **Factual accuracy** — every factual statement matches its source, not just "sounds plausible."
- [ ] **Source support** — every claim can be traced to a specific source; nothing is asserted on the strength of "the AI said so."
- [ ] **Metric consistency** — the same number is the same everywhere it appears, across this piece and against previously published material.
- [ ] **Math** — percentages, totals, and derived stats actually compute from the numbers given.
- [ ] **Attribution** — quotes, data, and case studies are credited correctly, and to sources actually cleared for that use.
- [ ] **Brand and tone** — reads like the brand, not like generic AI output wearing the brand's colors.
- [ ] **Privacy and confidentiality** — no customer-identifying detail, internal-only information, or confidential material appears where it shouldn't.
- [ ] **Hallucinated citations** — every named source, study, or statistic actually exists and says what it's cited as saying. Don't assume a citation is real because it's formatted correctly.
- [ ] **Unsupported competitive claims** — anything measured against a competitor or "the market" is backed by a named, checkable source.
- [ ] **Legal / compliance escalation** — anything touching regulated claims, guarantees, or legal language gets routed to a qualified reviewer rather than cleared by whoever's running this checklist.

## How to use this

This is a check on the *output*, run alongside — not instead of — [Human Review Framework](./human-review-framework.md), which sets who needs to review and at what level of scrutiny. A piece can pass every item here and still need sign-off from a specific accountable person if it's high-risk.

For the specific process behind the claim-related items, see [Marketing Claim Quality Check](../skills/marketing-claim-quality-check.md).
